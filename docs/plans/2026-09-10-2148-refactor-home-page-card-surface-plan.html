Goal Capsule

Objective. Every card on the home page renders on one surface, so a person looking at Home sees one card system instead of three slightly different ones. A reviewer can check this from the rendered page and from before/after screenshots, and nothing else on the page moves.

Means. Adopt the surface the design system already declares (.surface-card: 8px corner, 1px hairline, elev-1) and the padding pair the other eleven pages already use (16px for a content card, 12px for a record row) — KTD1, KTD2.

Authority. The Product Contract wins on product behavior. A Key Technical Decision wins on implementation mechanism inside its cited requirements. An Implementation Unit overrides neither.

Stop conditions. Stop if a card's content, order, wording or data would have to change to make the surface apply. Stop if meeting the surface requires a new component, a new class family, or a change outside the files listed in the units.

Execution profile. Interactive, one worktree, no new dependency. Tail ownership. The user selects the next action at the plan handoff.

Product Contract

Summary

Bring the home page's cards onto the single card surface the portal already documents: the right rail rows, the "Needs you" empty state and its queue rows, the "Waiting on others" rows, and the "Start something" bar. The change is class-level and reversible. No content, order, wording, data or layout decision changes.

Problem Frame

The home page is the last screen that draws its own cards. Its rail and Celebrations card use .surface-card (8px). Its empty state, queue rows and quick-actions bar restate the surface by hand with rounded-xl (16px). NeedsYou.vue and QuickActions.vue each carry border border-outline-gray-1 bg-surface-white to rebuild what the class already provides.

The padding has drifted the same way. The same page shows p-5 (empty state), p-3 (queue row), px-4 py-3 (rail) and px-3 py-2.5 (waiting row). Eleven other pages already agree on one pair — p-4 for a card that contains content and p-3 for a row that represents one record — across 15 call sites. The home page is the outlier, not the reference.

Effects a person sees: corners that disagree by 8px between two cards stacked in the same column, and a rail whose rows sit on a different inset from the queue beside them.

Requirements

Six requirements. R1-R3 are the surface; R4-R5 protect everything else; R6 is the proof.
IDRequirement
R1. Every card on the home page renders with the same corner radius, the same 1px hairline border, and the same surface fill.
R2. Every home-page card uses one of two paddings: 16px on a card that contains content, 12px on a row that represents one record. A divided card carries its inset on its segments, not on its container. No other padding value appears on a home-page card.
R3. Every tappable home-page card row keeps a 44px minimum box.
R4. Every home-page card keeps its current content, order, wording, figures and trailing control.
R5. Nothing outside the card surface changes: the week spine, page layout and vertical rhythm, type scale, palette, elevation values and hover treatment stay as they are.
R6. An automated check reads the rendered home page and fails when a card is drawn off the shared surface.

Key Decisions

KD1 Card-surface consistency only. This plan does not fix the rail's figures, the per-role card shape, or the dead space under a short left column, even though all three are visible on the same screen. (session-settled: user-directed — chosen over fixing the mislabelled figures and the per-role rail shape, or both in one pass: the look and feel are approved and the request was to leave them alone.) Governs R1, R2, R3, R5.

Success Criteria

  • Every home-page card computes to an 8px corner radius, and to 12px or 16px padding wherever the card owns its inset, read off the rendered page rather than judged by eye.
  • A reviewer comparing before/after screenshots for all three identities sees only the deltas listed in the Visual Reference. No other pixel moves.
  • The change adds no component, no class family, no Tailwind utility, and no dependency.
  • The diff touches only the five files named in the Implementation Units.

Scope Boundaries

In scope. The card surface, padding and row-height floor of the four card groups on /: the right rail rows, the Celebrating-this-month card, the Needs-you empty state and queue rows, the Waiting-on-others rows, and the Start-something bar.

Out of scope. Card content, order, wording, figures and links. The week spine. Page layout, column widths and vertical rhythm. The palette, type scale, elevation values and motion. Every other page's cards.

Deferred to Follow-Up Work

Deferred — not this plan
  • Rail figure semantics. The Attendance card prints the highest-count status as its subtitle ("Holiday this month"), and the Leave-left figure covers only the first leave type while "+1 more" hides the rest. Deferred with KD1.
  • Per-user rail shape. Cards appear and disappear by role and by date, so the rail's height and the page's rhythm change per person. Deferred with KD1.
  • Dead space under a short left column. A quiet queue leaves several hundred pixels of empty paper between the queue and Start something. Deferred with KD1.
  • The week spine's 16px corner. WeekSpine.vue draws rounded-xl (16px) while .surface-field declares 12px — the same defect class on the same page, on the one region this plan was told not to touch.
  • The same card drift on other pages. Record only; no audit was run outside the home page.

Planning Contract

Key Technical Decisions

KTD1 Padding: adopt the repo-wide pair, p-4 for a content card and p-3 for a record row. The home page's p-5, px-4 py-3 and px-3 py-2.5 are the outliers against 15 call sites on eleven pages. (session-settled: user-approved — chosen over keeping each card's current padding: the user confirmed one shared padding scale, and research showed the pair is already the repo convention.) Governs R2.

KTD2 Corner: .surface-card's 8px is canonical; the rail is the reference, not the exception. The waiting rows already match it, and it is the documented vocabulary. (session-settled: user-approved — chosen over raising the rail's rows to 16px: that would keep the queue's current look but leave the design system's surface unapplied.) Governs R1.

KTD3 Apply the surface by adopting the class, not by restating utilities. Adopting .surface-card removes three hand-rolled declarations per card and makes the surface greppable. Rejected: a shared SurfaceCard.vue wrapper (three call sites, and a wrapper hides which surface a card sits on) and a new Tailwind utility (it would become a second way to say the same thing). Governs R1.

KTD4 Keep the rail's hover:border-blue-600 and the documented elev-1:hover lift. The interaction treatment is approved look and feel. Unifying the surface changes the resting shape only. Governs R5.

KTD5 Prove the surface with a computed-style check in frontend/tests/e2e/visual-foundation.spec.ts. Rejected: screenshot comparison (the file's own contract states there is none) and a source-level lint rule (it cannot tell a rendered card from an unused branch, and would fire on the deferred spine). Governs R6.

Unchanged on purpose

Divider colour between the quick-action segments stays divide-outline-gray-2. It matches the other divided cards in the app (Approvals, Payslips), and the card's own border already comes from .surface-card. Changing it would be a visible change with no requirement behind it.

Assumptions

  • rounded is 8px under frappe-ui's Tailwind preset, so the two skeleton blocks that stand in for cards can match the surface without the class. (docs/design-system.md records the preset: rounded 8px, rounded-md 10px, rounded-lg 12px, rounded-xl 16px.)
  • NeedsYou's loading prop is never passed by the home page. Its skeleton is still aligned, so no hand-rolled radius survives inside the card set.
  • The e2e fixture data is unchanged, so the manager home page still renders the single Waiting-on-others row after the change. That row keeps its shape under R4.
  • The spine placeholder in Dashboard.vue keeps rounded-xl because it stands in for the out-of-scope spine.

Sequencing

Before U1 lands, capture /helixhr/ at 1180px for the employee, manager and HR identities, plus 360px for the employee. The Visual Reference carries the employee's before frame only, so the other two have to be taken before the first unit changes the cards; afterwards they can only be produced by reverting finished work.

U1, U2 and U3 are independent and may land in any order. U4 depends on all three, because it asserts the surface across every card group. Nothing else in the plan waits on anything.

U4. Check the surface from the rendered page

Goal
A regression that draws a home-page card off the shared surface fails a test instead of reaching a screenshot.
Requirements
R6, R1, R2
Dependencies
U1, U2, U3
Files
frontend/tests/e2e/visual-foundation.spec.ts
Approach
  1. Add a test to the existing employee describe block, titled so the Verification Contract's -g "surface" filter matches it — the plan uses every home-page card is drawn by the shared surface. Read computed style off /helixhr/: every element carrying .surface-card inside main has a corner radius of 8px.
  2. Assert padding on the element that owns the inset, not on the container. Every .surface-card inside main that declares its own padding computes to exactly 12px or 16px on all four sides. The Start-something container declares none, and each of its three segments computes to 12px.
  3. Assert the anti-regression half in the same test: no element inside main on the home page has both a non-zero border width and a 16px computed radius. This is what fails if a card is redrawn by hand.
  4. Add the same radius assertion to the manager describe block, so the rail's per-role shape cannot reintroduce a variant.
  5. Reuse the file's existing shape: a computed-style read with a failure message that names the offending element, in the style of undersizedControls.

Scope the check to main, so the portaled dialog surface (which is 16px by design on a phone) is not caught. Pair the border-width condition with the radius condition rather than asserting "no 16px radius anywhere", so a future rounded-full avatar or pill on the home page does not fail the test for the wrong reason.

The divided card is an established shape here, not a new exception: Profile, Approvals, Attendance and Directory already put .surface-card on a container whose children own the inset.

Patterns to follow

undersizedControls and heldSkeletonStyle in the same file: gather findings, then assert the list is empty with the route and element in the message.

Test scenarios
  • Covers R1. On /helixhr/ as an employee, every .surface-card inside main computes to an 8px corner radius.
  • Covers R2. Every card that declares its own padding computes to 12px or 16px on all four sides, and the set of distinct padding values found is a subset of those two. The Start-something container declares none, and its three segments compute to 12px each.
  • Covers R1. No element inside main on /helixhr/ has a non-zero border width together with a 16px computed radius.
  • Edge case: the home page with an empty queue (the empty state and the Celebrating card are the cards whose padding is 16px, not 12px) still satisfies both checks. The employee fixture already renders this state.
  • Edge case: at a 360px viewport, the same radius and padding checks pass, so the mobile stack does not introduce a second card shape.
  • Covers R6. A manager signed in and loading /helixhr/ satisfies the radius check, including the single Waiting-on-others row and the row that renders no figure.
  • Guard the guard: temporarily reintroducing rounded-xl on the empty state fails the test with the element named. Confirm this by hand once while implementing, then revert it.
Verification

The new tests pass on the changed home page and fail if any one card's corner is returned to 16px. The rest of visual-foundation.spec.ts stays green.
